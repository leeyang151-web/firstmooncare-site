Postnatal carer at home

A steady pair of hands, at home

An experienced postnatal carer who comes to you in the weeks after birth: to hold the baby while you sleep, to keep the house turning over, and to be there at three in the morning.

What it could look like

Help with the baby, and with everything around the baby

A carer is not a visitor to entertain. They arrive, read the room, and quietly take on whatever is heaviest that day.

  • Settling and soothing the baby so you can sleep, shower or simply sit down
  • Nappies, baths and feeds, however you are feeding, following your lead, with an IBCLC-certified lactation consultant arranged when you want one
  • Simple warm meals, the kitchen kept going, the washing kept moving
  • Company, and an experienced ear for the small questions that arrive at odd hours
  • Time with older children, so they get attention too
  • A short note at the end of each visit: what happened, what might help tomorrow

How it might work

Days, nights, or both

We are testing three shapes with families. The details, including price, will be set with the people who answer the survey.

Day visits

Four to six hours in the daytime, one or more days a week, for as long as you need.

Overnights

Roughly ten in the evening to six in the morning. The carer settles the baby between feeds so you sleep in longer stretches.

The First Moon

A package across the first weeks at home, shaped around how each week goes, with the same carer where we can.

Who it is for

For any family who wants calm, practical help after a birth

  • Families who know zuò yuèzi and want it at home, with or without relatives nearby
  • Families who have never heard of the tradition and simply want to rest
  • Partners who want to help but need sleep too
  • Families anywhere in the UK, in whichever language they are most at ease in

Questions people ask

The honest answers, for now

Is this medical care?

No. A carer gives practical help and experienced company, not medical advice or treatment. Your NHS maternity team and GP remain responsible for your health and your baby's health, and a carer will always point you to them for anything medical.

Who would the carers be?

People with real experience of looking after newborns and new mothers, many with the tradition in their own families. Before any visit happens, every carer will have references taken up and the checks you would expect, and we will publish exactly what those are when the service opens.

Can I have the same carer each time?

That is the aim. Continuity is most of what makes this feel like care rather than a service, so we will plan around it wherever we can.

Where would you cover?

Across the UK, starting where the most families on the waitlist are. Tell us where you are in the survey; that is how we decide where to begin.

What would it cost?

We do not know yet, and we would rather not guess. The survey asks what a fair price would be for the shapes above, and we will set it with that in hand.

Can you help with breastfeeding?

A carer helps practically: bringing the baby for feeds, pillows and water, bottle prep and sterilising, setting up a pump, keeping notes. Feeding advice itself comes from an IBCLC-certified lactation consultant, contracted by First Moon, who visits at home and leaves a plan your carer follows between visits.

First Moon offers rest, food and practical help. It is not a medical service. Your NHS maternity team and GP remain responsible for your health and your baby’s health.
